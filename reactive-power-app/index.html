Otherwise, leave Reactive capability blank to derive it from the apparent power and power factor (√(S² − P²)), or enter the manufacturer's value directly. A capacitor bank supplies part of the reactive demand directly, so it reduces the share the inverters must cover. (The original worksheet added it to the demand — corrected here.)

STEP 5 Inverter P–Q capability (datasheet table)

Fill in the manufacturer's Q(t)-mode table: for each grid voltage, the maximum reactive power (% of rated kVA) at each active-power loading (p.u. of rated kVA). Leave a cell empty for N/A — operation not permitted there. Capacitive and inductive limits are assumed symmetric, as in the datasheet. The diagram below is drawn from these values.
The marker shows the design operating point: active power per inverter against the reactive power each inverter must supply (—). It must sit inside the capability curve of every voltage the plant has to support.

STEP 6 Reactive capability vs grid voltage

Grid codes require reactive support across a voltage band, not just at nominal. With the Step 5 table in use, each voltage level reads the inverter's capability straight from the table (interpolated between rows and loadings, clamped at the table's edges). Otherwise a current-limit model applies: apparent power shrinks with voltage below 1.00 p.u. (S = V × Srated) and holds rated above, with active power dispatched first.

Inverters are dispatched at the active power actually needed to deliver the grid limit — (limit + losses) ÷ count — as in a G99 study, so surplus inverters free reactive headroom. Where the capability table does not permit that loading at a voltage, active power is backed off to the maximum permitted point and the reactive capability there is used — the row then shows the reduced achievable P with a ⚠ and an amber status.

STEP 7 Grid-code compliance diagrams

The two assessment curves of a G99 Type C reactive power study. V–Q at Registered Capacity: the requirement polygon (dashed) against the plant's capability envelope at full output across the voltage range. P–Q at nominal voltage: the requirement envelope against plant capability over the whole output range. Capability is at the point of interconnection — net of transformer consumption on the export side, aided by it on the absorption side; inverter capability is taken as symmetric and the capacitor bank counts for export only.
